I study how schools, colleges, and public policies shape educational opportunity, using causal inference methods to evaluate career pathways, ethnic studies, free college, and financial support for community college students.

I am an Assistant Professor at the University of Pennsylvania Graduate School of Education and Faculty Research Director of the Office of School & Community Engagement. I received my Ph.D. in Economics of Education from Stanford University in 2019 and previously taught at the University of Massachusetts Amherst.

Research themes

Career pathways and the transition to college

How high school career and technical education investments change dropout, graduation, and college enrollment.

  • The Effects of At-Scale Career Pathway Investments on the Transition from High School to College (2025)
  • The Dropout Effects of Career Pathways: Evidence from California (2020)

Ethnic studies and culturally relevant education

Longer-run effects of ethnic studies courses on academic engagement, attainment, and civic participation.

  • Ethnic Studies Increases Longer-run Academic Engagement and Attainment (2021)
  • Cultural Relevance at Scale: The Effects of an Ethnic Studies Expansion on Academic Outcomes (2026)

College affordability and basic needs

Free college programs, cost-of-living grants, and the financial and mental health barriers community college students face.

  • Get a Skill, Get a Job, Get Ahead? Evaluating the Effects of Virginia's Workforce-Targeted Free College Program (2026)
  • The Role of Policy Design in Free College Programs: Evidence from Virginia (2025)
  • Mental Health and Basic Needs as Barriers to Persistence and Credit Accumulation in Community Colleges (2025)
